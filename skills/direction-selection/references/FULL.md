# Full comparison

Use after the [applicability router](../SKILL.md#applicability-router) selects `full`. Record the trigger and extend the shared decision loop only where the choice needs this additional work. Reuse valid framing, evidence, and candidate coverage.

## Challenge the frame

Separate the required outcome from a symptom or suggested mechanism. Reframe only when a credible alternative framing could change the solution space. Resolve upstream choices first; keep materially coupled choices together and independent choices separate. State the commitment needed now and which later decisions can remain open.

If the domain is too unfamiliar to establish the frame or candidate space, orient through [DISCOVERY.md](DISCOVERY.md). Identify the area needing understanding rather than predicting what research must discover.

## Establish candidate coverage

Start with known materially distinct, credible directions. State serious candidates neutrally before elaborating a favorite.

Examine the candidate space when the trigger is an omitted direction or shared premise, weak comparative justification for a consequential choice, or a best/ideal request with inadequate coverage. For new evidence, determine whether it changes ranking only, changes the frame, or exposes a candidate-space gap. Known competitors alone do not require another candidate class.

For a required examination, establish which relevant families and sources were considered, why exclusions hold, and whether credible unresolved leads could alter the decision. Investigate simpler formulations, changed responsibility boundaries, avoided decisions, or coherent hybrids when they plausibly remove the tradeoff. An imaginable alternative is not itself a coverage defect; a hybrid must justify the complexity it adds.

Coverage is adequate when the relevant families and material leads have been addressed enough to support this commitment. A completed search count is not evidence of coverage. Preserve established results while investigating an actual omission or changed premise.

## Compare complete outcome paths

When options change responsibilities or workflow steps, trace each from the same representative input and starting state to the required usable outcome. Include material integration, manual steps, handoffs, recovery, and surrounding work. A component-only alternative needs the rest of its usable path included in the comparison.

Mark untested steps as assumptions. Check outcome quality and lifecycle cost on comparable paths; success of individual components does not establish end-to-end suitability.

## Challenge the recommendation

For the evidence challenge required by the main loop, examine realistic failure conditions, contrary results, and relevant transition, maintenance, operation, security, or compatibility costs. Identify what could make the strongest alternative preferable. Record which evidence supports, limits, or overturns the deciding claims; reuse challenges already resolved by applicable evidence.

When sunk effort, incumbent attachment, or exploratory lock-in may bias the choice, ask whether the same direction would be chosen from scratch with today's knowledge. Then account for legitimate future switching obligations under the main loop's cost rules.

Use an available, authorized independent second look only when its expected decision value justifies the cost. Ask neutrally and assess the returned evidence; reviewer agreement alone does not verify a claim.

When findings change the leader, update only affected comparison. When they change framing or coverage, return to that affected step. Finish through the shared support standard and output contract rather than starting another review cycle.
